AGRICULTURAL TENANCIES: the expression agricultural holding has a distinct legal meaning, describing land let under the Agricultural Holdings Act 1986, and that meaning is separate from the description of this company as an agricultural holding company. WHICH STATUTE GOVERNS A TENANCY DETERMINES ITS SECURITY, ITS SUCCESSION AND ITS VALUE. A tenancy under the Agricultural Holdings Act 1986, broadly one granted before 1 September 1995, generally carries security of tenure for the tenant's lifetime together with statutory rights of succession on death or retirement for up to two successions, subject to eligibility and suitability being established before the appropriate tribunal. A farm business tenancy under the Agricultural Tenancies Act 1995, broadly one granted on or after that date, carries no security of tenure beyond its contractual term and no rights of succession, terminating on notice of between twelve and twenty-four months where the term exceeds two years. LAND SUBJECT TO A 1986 ACT TENANCY IS COMMONLY WORTH MATERIALLY LESS THAN THE SAME LAND WITH VACANT POSSESSION, and any person dealing with agricultural land should establish which regime applies before relying on any valuation.
